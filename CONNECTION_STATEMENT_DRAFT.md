## Draft essay

My game, *Fahrenheit 451: The Fireman’s Choice*, focuses on how people can lose the ability to think for themselves when they stop paying attention. In Bradbury’s novel, censorship involves both government force and people choosing entertainment over difficult ideas. I wanted the player to experience that problem through actions instead of only reading an explanation.

The game starts with Montag doing his job willingly. The player soaks book piles and lights the fire before meeting Clarisse. This matters because Montag does not begin the novel as a rebel. His early satisfaction makes his later change more noticeable. The novel opens, “It was a pleasure to burn” (Bradbury __). Clarisse’s questions then interrupt that routine. Her conversation asks the player to stop and notice ordinary things, such as the moon and the grass, rather than rush toward the next task.

Mildred’s parlor walls show a different kind of control. The player can inspect the screens and repeatedly try to talk to her, but getting her attention is difficult. Montag can only ask, “Will you turn the parlor off?” (Bradbury __). I used repetition here because Montag’s frustration should be something the player experiences. The subway recall challenge develops the same idea: a jingle competes with words Montag is trying to remember. Wrong orders can be retried, so the challenge represents distraction without punishing slow readers.

Near the end, the river changes the pace and the book people give memory a practical purpose. They preserve ideas by remembering them together. The novel ends with Montag looking ahead: “When we reach the city” (Bradbury __). The player can return to the journal to connect each challenge with a specific event. The final choice connects these events. Preservation follows the direction of the novel, while Defiance and Submission are clearly marked adaptations. The city is destroyed in every version; the choice concerns Montag’s response afterward. This keeps the ending from suggesting that one decision could prevent the war. I want players to leave thinking about whether they would preserve difficult knowledge, use it angrily, or turn away from it.
